Sunday

9:42 PM

I was sitting in his car in the parking lot of my apartment complex. I clenched the folded piece of paper in my hand, a page torn from my journal. I lingered before opening the door. I knew this might be the last time we spoke the way we always had, the last chance to ask another question or hear another story about him. Once the note was in his hands, there was no going back.

I thanked him, handed him the note, and rushed inside. 

The note said what I hadn't been brave enough to say out loud. It told him that I thought he was smart, kind, funny, and handsome; that I'd developed a crush on him. That I wanted to get to know him outside of work. 

He sent me a text when he got home.

He thanked me for the note. He was kind. He was gentle. 

He did not feel the same way.

I've never been on this side of the story before. I have always been the one waiting to be chosen, not the one taking the risk. I'm afraid I may have just messed up an easy friendship. A good connection. Maybe that was the last conversation we'd ever have. I still wanted to tell him about my new job. I wanted to hear where life would take him. I wanted our story to continue, even if only as friends.

But these were always the stakes. I knew what I was risking; I knew the trade offs.

You don't get honesty without vulnerability. You don't get clarity without risking disappointment. You don't get love without a little bit of embarrassment. 

I'm proud of myself for choosing honesty anyway.

I hope he never feels tricked by our friendship. I hope he doesn't revisit old conversations for hidden meanings or wonder whether he accidentally led me on. I really tried to keep my feelings from impacting our professional relationship - they were my feelings to carry, not his.

Until they weren't.

In a strange way, I feel lighter. A corner of my mind has gone quiet. There is no more searching for evidence that never existed, no more comparing myself to the imaginary women who he might like more. There is no more "maybe". There is an answer, and there's a peace that comes with that. I will still think fondly on my memories with him.

He's the first person I've genuinely liked since my last relationship. He is proof that I am capable of wanting someone again, and for that I am grateful.

As I think back on the people I've loved and the people I've nearly loved, I realize that they were all mirrors of the person I was becoming. As my values and needs evolved, so did the people I was drawn to.

At one point, I was drawn to excitement. At another, familiarity. I confused being needed for being loved. I confused intensity and intimacy.

This time, I was drawn to laughter. To joy. To safety, security, and emotional stability.

He felt like the kind of person I hope I continue to choose. I think that's the transformation. Not that he chose me, but that I chose differently.

I chose differently.

A year ago, I would have believed that someone like him was out of my league. This year, I thought he was someone worth getting to know. 

And I've also been thinking about how lucky I am to live in this moment in history.

For a long time, women's romantic lives were shaped more by circumstance than by choice. Economics decided. Politics decided. Families decided. Marriage was less about affection, less about love, and more about survival.

But my life is different.

I get to choose who I love.

I get to tell someone that they're beautiful.

I get to ask.

I get to hear "no".

Heartbreak is painful. But it is also evidence of a freedom that so many women before me never had. I would rather risk rejection than spend my life wondering who I might have become if I'd been brave enough to speak.

Like every person I've cared about before, he became part of the woman I am becoming. He asked something new of me.

And? 

There will be other people.

There will be other versions of me, and I hope I remember this kindly because she stopped waiting to be chosen.

She chose.

And to choose is a radical act.
