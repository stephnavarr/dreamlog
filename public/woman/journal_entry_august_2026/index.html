[DRAFT] Journal Entry from August 2, 2026: Asking Questions

On bravery, and on the freedom to choose.

Sunday

9:42 PM

For a long time, women’s romantic lives were shaped more by circumstance than by choice.

Economics decided. Politics decided. Families decided. Marriage was less about affection, compatibility, or love, and more about survival.

But my life is different.

I get to choose who I love.

I get to tell someone that they’re beautiful.

I get to ask the question.

I get to hear “no”.

This is the story of my latest “no”.

I was sitting in his car in the parking lot of my apartment complex. I clenched the folded piece of paper in my hand, a page torn from my journal. I knew this might be the last time we spoke the way we always had, the last chance to ask another question or hear another story about him. Once the note was in his hands, there was no going back.

I thanked him for the ride, handed him the note, and rushed inside.

The note said what I hadn’t been brave enough to say out loud. It told him that I thought he was smart, kind, funny, and handsome. That I’d developed a crush on him.

My phone lit up with a text from him about an hour later. I took a deep breath and opened the message.

He thanked me for the note. He was kind. He was gentle.

He did not feel the same way.

I locked my phone and sat in silence for a while before I replied, thanking him and wishing him well.

I’ve never been on this side of the story before.

Historically, I’ve been the one waiting to be chosen by an invisible, shadowy concept of a person that lived more in my imagination than in my real life. This time, I took matters into my own hands. I was intrigued by him and I went for it. Unfortunately, that’s where this story ends.

I knew that to get the clarity I craved I needed to be vulnerable and risk disappointment. You don’t get love without a little bit of embarrassment.

I’d written the note a few days beforehand, and it’d sat on my dresser, where it would stare back at me every morning. What would I be admitting – to myself, to the world – if I handed it over? That I was open to love? That I wanted to bring romance back into my life after months of tucking that version of myself deep into the darkest parts of my chest?

I don’t know what changed before dinner that night, as I read the note back once more before tucking it into my purse. Maybe I simply grew tired of waiting for something to happen.

I’m afraid I messed up an easy friendship, or at the very least a good professional connection. Maybe that was the last conversation we’ll ever have.

But these were always the stakes. I knew what I was risking. I knew the trade offs.

Crushing on him showed me that I might not be looking for a relationship, but I’m open to one with the right person. And that I’m willing to put it all out there for them.

Truthfully, the hardest things to accept in all of this are the things I can’t control. I can only hope he never feels tricked by our friendship. I can only hope he doesn’t revisit old conversations for hidden meanings or wonder whether he accidentally led me on. I did all I could to draw a line between my feelings and my actions. They were mine to carry, not his.

Until they weren’t.

All in all, I feel lighter. A corner of my mind has gone quiet. There’s no more searching for evidence, no more imagined realities. No more wondering if he meant something he didn’t.

No more “maybe”.

He’s the first person I’ve genuinely liked since my last relationship. In a way he’s proof that I’m capable of wanting someone again, that I might be capable of loving someone again one day.

As I think back on the people I’ve loved and the people I’ve nearly loved, I realize that they were all mirrors of the person I was becoming. As my values and needs evolve, so do the people I am drawn to.

The transformation this time around is not that he chose me (although that would’ve been nice), but that I chose differently.

A year ago, I would have believed that someone like him was out of my league. This year, I just thought he was someone worth getting to know.

Heartbreak is painful. But it is also evidence of a freedom that so many women before me never had. I would rather risk rejection than spend my life wondering who I might have become if I’d been brave enough to speak.

Like every person I’ve cared about before, he became part of the woman I am becoming. He asked something new of me.

There will be other people and other versions of me. I hope I remember this version of myself kindly because she decided to choose instead of waiting to be chosen.

She was brave enough to ask the question.

She was brave enough to hear the answer.
